Miskatonic Professor Found Dead Amid Queer Papers

Notes of the Late Dr. Angell Mention a “Cult of Cthulhu” — Police Baffled by Statuette of Outlandish Design — Servants Report Whippoorwills Gathered at the Window — Coroner Returns Verdict of Heart Failure

The Cthulhu Papers

Arkham, Nov. 8. — Dr. George Gammell Angell, Professor Emeritus of Semitic Languages in Brown University, and an authority of world-wide repute upon the inscriptions of antiquity, was discovered dead in the parlour of his residence in Williams Street shortly before seven o’clock this morning. The professor had attained his ninety-second year, and Dr. Hennessey, the coroner, has returned a verdict of death by failure of the heart; yet there are circumstances attending the gentleman’s decease so singular that we should fail in our duty to our readers did we not set them down.

The professor was found seated at his writing-desk, before him a heap of manuscript in his own hand, together with a small bas-relief of a grey-green stone, representing a monster of a form at once suggestive of an octopus, a dragon, and a human caricature — a thing which no school of sculpture, ancient or modern, savage or civilised, has ever owned. Upon the uppermost sheet of the manuscript was written, in a hand which the attending physician describes as “shaken beyond all recognition,” the single word: CTHULHU.

A tin dispatch-box, securely locked, was taken charge of by the police, who decline to make any statement concerning its contents. A servant, who gave her name as Hannah M——, declares that a great company of whippoorwills gathered upon the window-ledge at the very moment of the professor’s death, and “sung until the soul went out of him” — whatever that may signify.

The late professor had been engaged, since the winter of 1925, upon a work which he described to his colleagues as “a key to certain myth-patterns of the utmost antiquity.” Among his papers, our reporter is credibly informed, occurs repeatedly the outlandish sentence, “Ph’nglui mglw’nafh Cthulhu R’lyeh wgah’nagl fhtagn,” which a learned correspondent of the Ledger renders, with every reservation, as: “In his house at R’lyeh dead Cthulhu waits dreaming.”

What manner of dream may kill a hale gentleman of ninety-two, our readers must judge for themselves.

What Is “Cthulhu”?

The name occurs in no dictionary, cyclopædia, or gazetteer to which the Ledger has access. It is recalled, however, that in the spring of 1908 an inspector of the New Orleans police, Mr. J. R. Legrasse, exhibited before the American Archaeological Society a statuette of similar outlandish design, taken in a raid upon a supposed cult in the swamps south of that city. The learned gentlemen assembled upon that occasion were unable to identify the workmanship, and the matter was dropped from the public prints.

The Weather

To-night — Fog upon the river; unseasonable cold. To-morrow — Rain, with queer lights probable in the north. Whippoorwills in unusual numbers.

Strange Lights Upon the Miskatonic

Dunwich, Nov. 8. — Farmers along the upper Miskatonic report having observed, upon three successive nights, a luminous vapour of a colour which they profess themselves unable to name, hovering above the river in the neighbourhood of the old Whateley place. The selectmen have been petitioned to “do something,” though what, precisely, is not stated.

Dunwich Folk Bar Their Doors at Dusk

The inhabitants of Dunwich have taken to barring their doors at dusk, and no child is permitted abroad after sundown. Old Whateley, whose eccentricities have long afforded matter for gossip, was heard to remark in the general store that “they be a-comin’ fer somebody sune,” and would say no more.

Innsmouth Bus Line Suspended

The Arkham & Innsmouth Motor Coach Company announces the suspension of its service, owing — it is said — to the condition of the road. Persons having business in Innsmouth are advised to postpone it. The hall of the Esoteric Order of Dagon remains open, though its members are not disposed to converse with strangers.

Late Bulletin

New Orleans, Nov. 8 (by wire). — Inspector Legrasse, retired, when shewn a photograph of the Angell statuette, is reported to have turned pale, and to have refused, in the most emphatic terms, to discuss the events of 1908.

One volume, bound in black leather, entitled Necronomicon (Latin text, Olaus Wormius). Liberal reward; no questions. Return to the Reading Room, Miskatonic Library.

University to Exhibit South Sea Curiosities

The Museum of Miskatonic University will open to the public, upon the fifteenth instant, an exhibition of antique jewellery and graven images recovered from the South Seas. The curator requests that visitors refrain from reading aloud any inscription they may happen to find.

Verdict in the Marsh Case

The coroner’s jury in the matter of the late Mr. O. Marsh, of Innsmouth, has returned a verdict of death by misadventure. The deceased was discovered upon the ledge known as Devil’s Reef, at low tide; the expression of his countenance is described by witnesses as “not to be described.”

Poultry Missing at Aylesbury

Residents along the Aylesbury Pike report the disappearance of fowls in considerable number. Tracks of an unusual pattern — as of a thing walking upon two legs, and yet not a man — were observed in the mud of the barnyards, and were by order of the constable obliterated before our photographer could arrive.
