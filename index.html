Telephone Disturbance Baffles Officials

Arkham, Nov. 3. — Residents throughout Arkham have become increasingly concerned by a peculiar disturbance affecting the local telephone exchange, wherein subscribers lifting their receivers are met not by the expected operator or dial tone, but by what witnesses describe as a low and rhythmic chanting. The phenomenon, reported from French Hill, Rivertown, and the vicinity of Miskatonic University alike, is said to persist for no less than thirty seconds and sometimes as long as a minute before ceasing without warning.

The Arkham Telephone Service acknowledges the occurrence, though company officials insist there is no cause for alarm. In a statement issued Thursday evening, representatives described the matter as merely an “intermittent technical irregularity,” and assured subscribers that engineers are conducting a thorough investigation. Service, the company further notes, remains otherwise unaffected, and no danger to the public has been identified.

Descriptions of the sounds vary considerably. Some subscribers liken the voices to a distant church litany, while others report a number of deep voices repeating unfamiliar syllables in unison. Miss Eleanor Price of Garrison Street informed our reporter that the chanting “sounded as though several men were speaking together in some foreign tongue,” while Mr. Walter Briggs, a bookseller of Old Arkham, compared the effect to “a choir heard across a great body of water.”

Despite repeated inspections of switching apparatus and trunk lines, company technicians have thus far discovered no fault capable of producing the disturbance. One engineer, who requested that his name be withheld, admitted that routine explanations such as crossed wires and radio interference have failed to account for several documented cases. The company, nevertheless, maintains that a satisfactory technical explanation will soon be found.

Meanwhile, speculation has flourished throughout the town. Students at Miskatonic University have proposed that experimental wireless equipment may be responsible, while others point toward unusual atmospheric conditions. A smaller number of residents have connected the chants with persistent local legends concerning isolated districts west of town. Such suggestions have been firmly dismissed by both municipal authorities and telephone officials.

Subscribers encountering the phenomenon are advised simply to remain upon the line until normal service resumes. Investigators are presently collecting reports concerning dates, locations, and durations of incidents in hopes of determining a common source. Until that source is identified, the strange chanting remains one of the more curious mysteries presently confronting the citizens of Arkham.

Fire Forces Evacuation at La Bella Luna

ARKHAM, Mass. — Patrons of La Bella Luna were ushered safely into the street Tuesday evening after a fire broke out within the popular restaurant's kitchen. Fire crews responded swiftly and brought the blaze under control before it could spread to neighboring establishments. Fire Marshal James Whitaker reported that no diners or employees were injured during the evacuation, which witnesses described as orderly and calm.

The owners of La Bella Luna expressed gratitude to the Arkham Fire Department and assured customers that the damage was largely limited to smoke and minor fire effects. Cleaning and repairs are already underway, and management stated that the restaurant is expected to be fully restored and ready to welcome guests again by the following week. Regular patrons of the establishment may therefore look forward to the return of their favorite Italian fare in short order.

The Weather

Strange Lights Upon the Miskatonic

Dunwich, Nov. 8. — Farmers along the upper Miskatonic report having observed, upon three successive nights, a luminous vapour of a colour which they profess themselves unable to name, hovering above the river in the neighbourhood of the old Whateley place. The selectmen have been petitioned to “do something,” though what, precisely, is not stated.

Dunwich Folk Bar Their Doors at Dusk

The inhabitants of Dunwich have taken to barring their doors at dusk, and no child is permitted abroad after sundown. Old Whateley, whose eccentricities have long afforded matter for gossip, was heard to remark in the general store that “they be a-comin’ fer somebody sune,” and would say no more.

Innsmouth Bus Line Suspended

The Arkham & Innsmouth Motor Coach Company announces the suspension of its service, owing — it is said — to the condition of the road. Persons having business in Innsmouth are advised to postpone it. The hall of the Esoteric Order of Dagon remains open, though its members are not disposed to converse with strangers.

Late Bulletin

New Orleans, Nov. 8 (by wire). — Inspector Legrasse, retired, when shewn a photograph of the Angell statuette, is reported to have turned pale, and to have refused, in the most emphatic terms, to discuss the events of 1908.

University to Exhibit South Sea Curiosities

The Museum of Miskatonic University will open to the public, upon the fifteenth instant, an exhibition of antique jewellery and graven images recovered from the South Seas. The curator requests that visitors refrain from reading aloud any inscription they may happen to find.

Verdict in the Marsh Case

The coroner’s jury in the matter of the late Mr. O. Marsh, of Innsmouth, has returned a verdict of death by misadventure. The deceased was discovered upon the ledge known as Devil’s Reef, at low tide; the expression of his countenance is described by witnesses as “not to be described.”

Poultry Missing at Aylesbury

Residents along the Aylesbury Pike report the disappearance of fowls in considerable number. Tracks of an unusual pattern — as of a thing walking upon two legs, and yet not a man — were observed in the mud of the barnyards, and were by order of the constable obliterated before our photographer could arrive.
